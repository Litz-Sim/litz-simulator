# Part art

Each file is the art for one catalog part: `<type>.svg`, with `--<variant>` for a
state or attribute value. The file's `viewBox` is its placement — the art draws
at those local units, and the part's pins must fall inside it (checked by
`art.spec.ts`).

## Fritzing assets

Some files are breadboard views taken from [Fritzing](https://fritzing.org)
(`fritzing-parts`), redrawn into this project's units (0.1 inch = 20 units) and
positioned so each catalog pin sits on the matching Fritzing connector. Those
files, and the one SVG they wrap, are licensed CC BY-SA 3.0 by the Fritzing
project — see `LICENSE.txt` in the `fritzing-parts` repository. Keeping them in
this project does not change the license of the rest of the simulator.

Generated by `build/fritzing/tools/convert.py`; the matching pin coordinates
land in `src/lib/parts/fritzing-geometry.ts`. Files without an entry there are
hand-drawn.

## Development boards

The ESP32 board art comes from the manufacturer's dimension drawing, not from
Fritzing: the C3 is traced from the CAD plot itself, and the C6 boards and the
P4 are drawn from the drawing's measurements (PCB size, header columns 22.86 mm
apart, 2.54 mm pitch, module width and antenna overhang; the P4 is 88 x 74 mm
with a 2 x 20 header along its top edge and the S31 coreboard 65 x 55 mm with
2 x 19). Header pads sit at the drawing's
coordinates so the pins in `library/board-parts.ts` land on them — a board whose
pads are not in two columns uses `pinnedBoard`, which takes each pin's x and y.
Drawings live in `build/refs/`; `build/refs/tools/board_art.py` regenerates them
and `board_from_pdf.py` converts a `pdftocairo` export into art.

The Arduino Uno, Nano and Pro Mini are drawn the same way from Arduino's
datasheet drawings (68.58 x 53.34, 43.18 x 17.78 and 33.02 x 17.78 mm), and the
Pico from the Raspberry Pi datasheet (51 x 21 mm, 40 pads on a 2.54 mm pitch).
`build/refs/tools/arduino_art.py` regenerates those four; the sheets are in
`build/refs/arduino/` and the Pico PDF in `build/refs/`.

## Modules

The sensor and storage breakouts were also redrawn, because their Fritzing
sources declare a size in px that the converter reads as a thousandth of an
inch: those boards came out a few millimetres wide with their pads a fraction
of a millimetre apart. `build/refs/tools/module_art.py` draws them from the
product dimensions (BME/BMP/BME680 20.3 x 17.8, DS1307 38 x 22, microSD
42 x 24, soil probe 20 x 60, NEO-6M 25 x 35 mm; DS18B20 as a TO-92), with the
header on a 2.54 mm pitch. The same script covers the parts that had the same
defect: the LM393 and PC817 in DIP-8 and DIP-4, the 8-position DIP switch and
the 10-segment bar graph, the A4988 carrier (20.3 x 15.2 mm, two 8-pin rows),
the 5 mm IR LED and photodiode, the coin motor and the 5 x 20 mm fuse.
`modules-art.spec.ts` holds their pins to the pads the art draws.

The servo, HX711 and DS3231 keep their Fritzing art, which is already at world
scale; only their pins were moved onto the holes and wire ends that drawing
actually has (the HX711 and DS3231 at the drilled header holes, the servo at
its three lead ends).

## Header pads

A gold pad is a solder point, so it lies on the PCB: what may stick out past
the outline is the pin, never the pad. Breakouts whose Fritzing art hung the
pad off the board at the end of a header leg keep their pin coordinates and
grew the board out to the pad row instead. The dev boards are the exception —
their pads sit on the outline, as the header does on the real part.
`art.spec.ts` fails if a pad's centre leaves the board rect.

## Live art

Displays and controls that show a running value — LCD, OLED, e-paper, Nokia,
7-segment, potentiometers — are drawn by hand around `data-overlay` markers
(see `overlays.ts`). Each marker is the patch of screen, digit, knob or slider
the engine paints at runtime. These parts are never taken from Fritzing: a
static part photo would freeze the value. `art.spec.ts` fails if one of them
loses its marker, and the converter skips the same list.
